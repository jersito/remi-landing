Best overall, free AI chat logging

Pippy

Free tier: AI chat, sharing, nap predictionsPremium: $49.99/yriOS & Android

Pippy is our pick for parents who find Baby Daybook too heavy. You do not pick an activity, then a type, then a time. You just type or say it: "nursed 12 min left, 8 right" or "blowout diaper, changed outfit." Pippy files it in the right place.

The honest tradeoff: Baby Daybook tracks more kinds of things, like potty training and teething, and keeps a longer free history. If you want a log of everything, Daybook is deeper. If you want speed, Pippy is faster.

2
Best free tap-button tracker

Baby Tracker (Nighp Software)

Free core tracking with adsRemove Ads: $4.99 one-timePlus: $5.99/mo or $49.99/yr

Baby Tracker by Nighp Software is the closest free match to Baby Daybook's style. It is one of the most rated baby trackers on the App Store and covers the same core categories with simple buttons and timers.

Core tracking for feeds, sleep, diapers, pumping, growth, health, and milestones is free, with ads. A one-time $4.99 purchase removes the ads. The newer Baby Tracker Plus subscription adds What's Next, on-device AI timing cues for the next nap, feed, and diaper check. It supports Apple Watch, widgets, Live Activities, Siri Shortcuts, and PDF reports.

It has fewer activity types than Baby Daybook, and the design is plain. Its new Plus tier means the smart cues are paid.

Best for: parents who want free, familiar button logging Not for: anyone who dislikes ads

Pros

Cons

3
Best for sleep predictions

Huckleberry

Free tier: basic loggingPlus: about $68.99/yrPremium: $119.99/yr

Huckleberry is the app to pick if naps are the hard part. Its SweetSpot feature predicts the best time for the next nap or bedtime, and it has one of the bigger sleep content libraries.

The free tier covers basic logging, reports, and syncing across caregivers' devices. SweetSpot nap and bedtime predictions, Insights, and AI logging by text, voice, or photo sit in Plus, which Huckleberry's pricing page lists at $5.74 a month billed yearly. Premium, at $9.99 a month billed yearly, adds personalized sleep plans and Berry, an AI chat for parenting questions.

If you are leaving Baby Daybook to save money, note that Huckleberry's best features also cost money. See our Huckleberry alternatives guide for more.

Best for: families where sleep is the main struggle Not for: parents who want smart features for free

7-day free trial$9.99/mo or $99.99 lifetimeiOS & Android

Nara Baby is the pretty, calm option. It tracks feeds, pumping, sleep, diapers, growth, milestones, and medications, plus pregnancy and postpartum health for the parent.

Where Baby Daybook splits features across tiers, Nara has one plan that includes everything. Our Nara Baby alternatives guide covers the pricing change in detail.

Best for Apple Watch households

Sprout Baby Tracker

Premium trialPremium: $59.99/yr listedApple Watch app

Sprout Baby Tracker has been around since the early App Store days and it shows in the polish. Its Apple Watch app was featured in Apple's WWDC 2024 keynote, according to Sprout.

Sprout covers feeding, sleep, diapers, pumping, growth with WHO and CDC charts, milestones, health records, and moods, and it predicts the next nap with its Sleep Forecast feature. We could not confirm exactly which features stay free once the trial ends, so check the paywall in the app.

It is the right pick if most of your logging happens on your wrist.

Honest take

Where Baby Daybook still wins

We make a competing app, and we still think Baby Daybook is the better fit for some families.

Stay with Baby Daybook if

  • You want to log everything. Potty training, teething, symptoms, doctor visits, custom activities. Few apps match the range.
  • You rely on free growth charts. WHO and CDC curves, now on the free tier.
  • You already pay for Premium. It is shared with every caregiver on the profile, so the whole family is covered.
  • You want a lifetime plan with deep stats. $99.99 once for detailed charts is fair.

Switch if

  • You want family sharing without paying
  • You want nap predictions on a free tier
  • You would rather type or say one sentence than fill in forms
  • You only care about feeds, sleep, and diapers

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about Baby Daybook alternatives

Quick answers to what parents ask us most.

What is the best Baby Daybook alternative?

For most parents, Pippy. Family sharing and nap predictions, which Baby Daybook keeps in Premium, are free in Pippy, and you log by typing or saying one sentence instead of filling forms. If you want classic buttons for free, Baby Tracker by Nighp Software is the best free option.

Is Baby Daybook free?

Baby Daybook is free to download with a basic tier, and growth charts are now free. Family sync, sleep predictions, advanced statistics, unlimited history, custom reminders, and PDF export are part of Baby Daybook Premium. The US App Store lists Premium at $59.99 per year and a lifetime option at $99.99, with promotional prices sometimes shown.

Does Baby Daybook let me share with my partner for free?

Not fully. Baby Daybook lists family sync as a Premium benefit, though once you pay, Premium is shared with every caregiver on your baby's profile. Pippy includes family sharing on its free tier.

Can I move my data from Baby Daybook to another app?

Baby Daybook Premium can export your history to PDF, which is great for your records and your pediatrician. Most trackers, Pippy included, cannot import another app's history, so the usual approach is to export a PDF, then log the last few days in the new app to give it context.

Which Baby Daybook alternative is best for sleep?

Huckleberry is the best known for sleep with SweetSpot predictions in its paid Plus plan. Sprout has a Sleep Forecast feature. Pippy includes nap predictions on its free tier, which covers what most parents use day to day.
